Welcome! It's great to have you here. 

Every codebase starts somewhere, usually with a single `git commit -m "initial commit"`. That is why I wanted this first post to carry the exact same name. It marks the starting point of my personal devlog: a casual space where I document what I am building, what I am learning, and what breaks along the way.

Here is a quick look at who I am, what I do daily, and what you will find in this space.

---

### What I Do Day-to-Day

I work as a software engineer with a strong focus on the frontend, primarily using **JavaScript, TypeScript, and React**, alongside backend work in **Node.js**. 

In my day-to-day work, my team and I build and maintain web applications with an emphasis on standardizing UI/UX. When you have multiple single-page applications (SPAs) across an organization, keeping them consistent is a challenge. To solve this, we maintain internal npm packages and shared design standards. This keeps user experiences unified and prevents teams from reinventing the wheel for every new feature.

Solving real-world architectural problems (like component reusability, package maintenance, and clean data flow) is something I genuinely enjoy.

---

### Building in Open Source

Outside of work, I love creating open-source tools. When you build for the community or for your own workflow, you get to experiment freely with new ideas.

Some areas I regularly build in include:
- **UI Component Utilities**: Making reusable, accessible UI elements simpler to integrate.
- **npm Packages**: Creating standalone modules that solve specific developer pain points.
- **Developer Workflows & CLI Utilities**: Automating repetitive developer chores right from the command line.

Writing software that saves another developer five minutes of frustration is one of the most rewarding parts of engineering.

---

### Stepping into Rust

Lately, I have been actively learning and exploring **Rust**. 

Coming from a TypeScript and Node.js background, Rust offers an exciting shift in perspective. The strict compiler, memory safety guarantees without a garbage collector, and predictable performance push you to think deeply about how systems manage resources.

Right now, my focus with Rust is twofold:
1. **Building standalone projects** to get comfortable with the language, borrow checker, and ecosystem.
2. **Exploring how Rust can integrate with TypeScript and React**, whether that means high-performance CLI utilities, native Node modules, or WebAssembly.

I am still learning, and I plan to share both the breakthroughs and the tricky parts of that learning curve here.
